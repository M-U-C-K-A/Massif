# Getting started
Massif makes Minecraft maps. Shape the land, paint it, decorate it, then write it to a Minecraft world.

## The home screen
The home screen opens with Massif. On the left are the ways in: **New map**, **From a height map**, **From a real place**, **Open a map…** (a `.massif` file), **Import from WorldPainter…** (a `.world` file), **Import a Minecraft world…** (a world's folder), and the maps opened lately. In the middle are the settings of a new map, with a preview of the whole map.
- **Size**: width × depth in blocks, in steps of 128. Type any number: when you press Enter or leave the field, it becomes the nearest step (5000 becomes 4992), and a note under it says so. The chain between them links the two sides: linked (at first), typing one sets the other to it, for a square map; click it to set them apart.
- **Height**: Minecraft's own (−64 to 320) or taller, up to 2032 blocks.
- **Land**: a landform (flat, plains, hills, mountains, islands) or a template of the terrain generator, and how the ground is painted.
- **Water**: its level, water or lava, and beaches along the shores.
- **Seed**: the same seed always gives the same land.
- **Blocks**: the block palette the terrains are made of: Vanilla, a palette of a mod read for this map, or one of your own: see Painting ▸ Block palettes.
Press **Create map** (or `Enter`). A `.massif`, a `.world` or a height map dropped on the window opens too.

**From a height map** reads a 16-bit PNG, JPEG, OpenEXR, `.r16` or `.r32`. An OpenEXR keeps its floats whole: the page shows the file's lowest and highest values and starts with black and white at those heights (within the world's limits). **File ▸ Export Height Map…** saves the map's surface as a 16-bit PNG or, picked in the dialog, an OpenEXR of each column's height in blocks (one 32-bit float channel `Y`), for Gaea, World Machine or Houdini.

**Samples**, on the left, opens a small map made from a template of the terrain generator (Alpine range, Archipelago, Floating mountains), its relief built in a few seconds, to explore at once.

## The tour
The first time a map is open, a short tour of six steps shows the window: the World and Terrain sides, the tools, Brush Settings, the 2D map, the 3D view, saving and exporting. **Next** and **Back** (or the arrow keys) move through it, **Skip tour** or `Esc` ends it. **Help ▸ Take the tour** shows it again.

Before it, the very first start asks **Coming from WorldPainter?** **Yes** opens the WorldPainter workspace, sets WorldPainter's keyboard shortcuts and shows a tour of where its tools are in Massif instead; **No** keeps Massif's layout and the tour above. See Coming from WorldPainter.

## A real place
**From a real place** on the home screen, or **File ▸ New World from a Real Place…**, makes a world of the relief of a place on the Earth; **Edit ▸ Relief of a Real Place…** puts one into the map open, into the selection by its weight or over the whole map. Its land is painted again from the new relief, the old map's painted terrains, biomes, covers and objects taken off (**Terrains ▸ Keep** keeps them and writes only heights and water). Over the whole map, **Change it** under **Map** makes the map another size in the same step (whole tiles of 128 blocks, up to 32,768 a side, around its middle; what is painted outside the new size goes); one undo brings the old map and size back. **Blocks** chooses the block palette, as in New map (Vanilla, your own, the loaded mods'): a new world is made of it; into the map, another palette than the map's switches the map's palette in the same step to undo. The coasts follow a smooth line through the data, with a narrow beach, a sea floor that deepens from the shore and no square pools; lakes the data gives flat are water at their own level; the sea gets ocean biomes by its depth and latitude, lakes river, the sand beach. A tile that cannot be downloaded is made from coarser data, and the status bar says how many were.
- **Source**: **Terrain Tiles**, the heights of the whole Earth, about 30 m apart, ocean floors included, downloaded as needed; or **A file** you downloaded: a GeoTIFF (its metres a pixel read from it), a 16-bit PNG or a raw `.r16` / `.r32` (say how many metres a pixel covers and what black and white stand for).
- **Place**: search a place by name (press `Enter`), or type its latitude and longitude ("45.83, 6.86"), or its bounds. Drag the small map to move, scroll to zoom; the orange outline is the map.
- **Size and scale**: the new world's width and depth (up to 32,768 blocks a side, about two minutes to make), and **Blocks a metre** (1: a block is a metre; 0.1: ten metres).
- **Heights**: **Exaggeration** makes the relief taller; **Sea level** is where the sea stands, and **Real height there** the real height that stands at it (0: the sea's; higher for a place inland, its valleys then filled). **Range** and **Lowest**, **Highest**: the heights the ground keeps to; **Squeeze into the range** brings mountains too tall (and seas too deep) into it rather than cutting them off.
The picture under the map shows the ground it makes and the heights it reaches. Gaps, spikes and pits of the data are filled from the heights around them; sand lies only along the water, snow only above the snow line of the latitude. Tiles are kept on disk: a place seen once works offline. The ground is painted by the automatic paint; in the map it is one step to undo. Elevation: Terrain Tiles by Mapzen on AWS Open Data (SRTM, GMTED2010, ETOPO1, 3DEP, EU-DEM and others; **All the sources** in the dialog lists them); search: Nominatim, © OpenStreetMap contributors.

Behind the home screen is the background chosen in **Settings ▸ Appearance ▸ Home background**: **Ridges** (ranges of mountains under a glow, made of dots, the default, with a **Palette**, **Height**, **Glow** and **Ranges**), **Waves** (smooth bands of colour rising and melting into each other, with a **Palette**, **Scale**, **Melt**, **Speed** and **Direction**), **Pixel** (the waves in large square pixels, with a **Pixel size**) or **Prism** (crisp columns of colour drawing a **Shape**, with a **Direction**, a **Motion** and their own palettes). A picture shows it alive above its settings. The words over it stay legible whatever you choose; over Waves, Pixel and Prism the new map's settings sit on a solid surface. **Animate the background** stops it, and so does Reduce motion.

## A Minecraft world
**File ▸ Import Minecraft World…** (or **Import a Minecraft world…** on the home screen) makes a map of a Minecraft Java world: pick its folder, or drop it, its `level.dat` or a region file on the window. Worlds of every version Massif exports are read, 1.12 and older to 26.x, and mods' blocks and biomes keep their ids. The dialog asks:
- **Dimension**, and the **Area**: the whole world, or drawn on the map of its chunks (drag; a click picks a region of 512 × 512 blocks); the **Heights** kept.
- **Above the ground**: **Keep it** keeps buildings, trees and overhangs block for block as sculpted chunks, so they come back unchanged on export; **Trees as layers** makes each kind of tree an object layer of the world's own trees, painted where they grew; **Drop it** brings the land alone.
- Plants, snow and carpets as ground covers; ground of mixed blocks as terrains mixing them; biomes; caves and the underground as sculpted chunks (much memory).
Each column gets the height of its ground, its top block as terrain (a terrain of the map with that block on top, or a new one in the **Imported** group), its water or lava, and its biome. The import report lists what came in, the terrains made and the blocks left out.

## Saving and exporting
- `⌘S` saves the map as a `.massif` file. The Finder and Explorer show the map as the file's picture.
- `⌘E` exports to Minecraft: the saves folder, the world's name, the version of the game, its border and data packs; in **Rules**, the game mode, the difficulty and the world rules.
- **Rules** writes the world's game rules, as each version names them, so that it opens as set with no `/gamerule` to type: a preset (**Creative / Frozen**: nothing spawns, spreads, grows or decays, random ticks at 0, no weather nor day going by, noon for good; **Survival safe**: fire does not spread, mobs break no blocks; **Vanilla**: the game's own), or each rule (mobs, patrols, the wandering trader, phantoms, mobs changing blocks, fire spreading, random ticks, keeping one's inventory, the day and the weather going by, the time of day and the weather). **Save as preset…** keeps a set of rules under your own name (listed in **Settings ▸ Export**); the map keeps the rules of its last export. Rules a version does not have are greyed and left out.
- From 1.18.2 to 26.3, the chunks, `level.dat` and the world's folders are written as that version writes them itself: it opens the world without converting it or asking for a backup. Blocks and biomes the version does not have yet become the closest ones it has (a cherry log a birch log, a pale garden a dark forest); renamed blocks take the version's name (short grass is grass before 1.20.3).
- The water is written as the game leaves it: flowing water at the edges of the steps and falls of rivers, so that no pool rises and nothing overflows when a player changes a block beside the water. Blocks of objects standing in water are waterlogged.
- **Test Export…** asks which part of the map to try — the selection (draw it with the Select tools first), around the middle of the map view, or blocks typed in — and writes the tiles of 128 blocks it reaches to a separate world, to check the result in the game quickly. **Regions** writes whole region files of 512 × 512 blocks instead, as WorldPainter's: **Pick on the map…**, then click or drag over the regions on the map (their grid and names shown), and **Done**; **Snap to regions** rounds the other parts out to whole regions. The dialog lists the `r.x.z.mca` files it writes.
- **File ▸ Share Image…** draws pictures of the map to post — **Diorama** (the map as a block), **Relief** (its most dramatic relief, the sun low), **Map** (from above, with a scale and a north arrow), **Map and relief** — with a band giving its name, size and version of Minecraft, which can be left out. Pick one in the carousel, then **Copy** it to paste into Discord or Reddit, or **Save PNG…**; 16:9, square or 4K.
- Picking **Paint biome** shows the map's biomes, **Paint terrain** its terrains; the view comes back when you change tools (**Settings ▸ Appearance ▸ Switch the map's view with the tool**).

## Autosave
- **Settings ▸ Export ▸ File names** sets the name every export is offered under (worlds, schematics, height maps, renders, animations, Share Image pictures): a template of `{name}`, `{kind}`, `{date}`, `{time}`, `{timestamp}`, `{counter}`, `{size}` and `{version}`, with ready ones, the date ISO, European or US and the time at 24 h or 12 h, and a preview. Characters a system refuses become a dash, and the dialog opens in the last export's folder with a name free there (`-2`, `-3` after it otherwise).
- While the map has changes not saved, Massif keeps a copy of it every few minutes (**Settings ▸ Autosave**: on or off, every 1 to 15 minutes), in a folder of its own, never over your file. It is written in the background: the map stays smooth, even a huge one.
- Should Massif close without saving (a crash, a forced quit, a power cut), the next start offers **Restore unsaved changes?**: the map's name and when its copy was taken. **Restore** opens it with changes to save; **Discard** deletes the copy; **Later** keeps it for the next start.
- Should Massif run into an error while you work, it writes a copy of the map at once and says so: **Save As…** keeps the map under a name of your own, **Carry On** goes back to work, **Quit** keeps the copy for the next start.
- Saving never harms the file already there: a full disk, a folder that cannot be written or a file held by OneDrive or an antivirus leaves it as it was, and the message says what to do.
- Saving the map, or closing it, removes its copy. At most 5 copies are kept.

## Save points
- **File ▸ Save Point…** keeps the map as it is now under a name, to come back to it. The **Versions** panel lists the points with a picture and their date.
- **Restore** puts the map back as it was then, as one step to undo. **Compare** shows on the 2D map where the land is higher or lower (accent) or painted differently (grey).
- In a point's **…** menu, **Open as a copy** opens it as a new map; **Open read-only** only shows it. **Rename…** and **Delete…** too.
- The points are kept in the `.massif` file and share its unchanged tiles: a point costs only what changed since. Older versions open the file as the map alone.
- The points are the map's **versions**, drawn as a **timeline**: the map's line on the left, a lane for each branch, curves where a branch leaves and is merged, a ring on a merge. Click one for its picture and **Restore**, **Compare**, **Open as a copy**, **Open read-only**, **Rename…**, **Delete…**. **Current** marks the one the map stands on.
- The **Versions** button at the right of the top bar names that version and opens the panel; a dot says that a contribution waits or is under review.

## Working together (beta)
One person, the **owner**, keeps the map; **contributors** start from a version of it and send their changes back as a small `.massifpatch` file. Everything is in the **Versions** panel.
- **Export my changes…** writes the tiles changed since a version, each with what it was, signed with your name and a message. **Import a contribution…** brings one in; a contribution to another map is refused, and the terrains and layers it uses are added to the map.
- **A zone through another tool**: **Export a zone…** (File ▸ Export Region…) with **Review it when it comes back** keeps a version and writes `zone.json` beside the images. Brought back with **Import a contribution…** (or Import Mask…, **Review…**), the images are painted onto that version and compared with it; tiles only a hair off through the heightmap are left out.
- **The review**: the contribution's **areas** (changed tiles touching each other) are drawn over the 2D map, green **new**, the accent **changed**, red **in conflict** (changed in the map too). **Accept**, **Reject**, or **Resolve…** an area in conflict: **Mine**, **Theirs** or **Tile by tile**, with pictures of each. The map's settings it changes are listed apart, each with a switch. **Apply** merges what was taken as one step to undo and makes a version.
- **A region for someone else**: **File ▸ Export Region…**, **Massif map** (rather than Images), writes the selection and a **Border** of tiles around it as a `.massif` of its own, at its coordinates, with the map's terrains, layers and versions; a version of the map is kept. The collaborator works on it in Massif and sends it back. **File ▸ Merge Massif Map…** opens the review: **The map shows: Before / After** draws their land over the tiles it would bring, outlined new, changed or in conflict. Only the region's tiles come back. A whole copy of the map, or another map, merges the same way.
- **A shared folder** (Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud, Git): **Shared folder ▸ Choose…**, then **Publish** puts the map in it for contributors to start from. Contributors open it from there (their work is saved as a map of their own) and **Send my changes…** into it; the owner sees the contributions waiting, with **Review** and **Set aside**.
- More in the guide: `docs/versioning.md`.

## Survival check
**Edit ▸ Analyse the map…** checks the whole map for a survival game, every block counted as the export writes it: the layers of the terrains, the rock and the bedrock under them, the water, what the covers grow and the objects. In red, what breaks survival: riches lying around (diamond blocks, ores at the surface, far more ore than Minecraft makes), bedrock above the floor, blocks no player can break, holes into the void, no wood. In amber, what is missing: ores (turned off, or no rock at their heights: **Set up the ores** opens Edit ▸ Ores), lava for a Nether portal, water, grass for animals, trees near the spawn. Then the way through a game, from wood to the Nether, what holds up, and **How to fix it**.
- **Relief**: the heights players stand at, the steps between columns (walked, climbed, falls that hurt, falls that kill), the water, overhangs and caves.
- **Blocks**: the ores, the blocks at the surface and in the ground, plants, objects. **Biomes**: the biomes as the export gives them.
- **Show on map** glides the map to the spot, the nearest to the spawn. **Copy report** copies it all as Markdown; the **…** menu saves it.
The check runs in the background — a few seconds for a map of 5,888 blocks a side — and comes back as it was while the map is the same.

## Ores
**Edit ▸ Ores…** sets the ores the export lays in the rock — Minecraft adds none to the map's finished chunks —, as the game lays them since 1.18 to begin with.
- Across the top, each ore with what the map will hold of it: click one to pick it (a funnel marks one kept to some biomes); the **+** at the end adds back an ore taken out. The map, small, at the left: click or drag on it to choose where to cut the map.
- On the left, the map cut through there, the ores exactly where the export will put them, and a **candle** for each band of an ore: the wick from the lowest height a vein starts at to the highest, the body where veins are the most common, its width the size of a vein. Drag a candle's ends or its body's edges to move those heights, its body to move the whole band, its side to make veins larger; the wheel over a body sets how many veins a chunk gets. Drag elsewhere to look along the cut.
- On the right, the ore expected at each height of the map: **Blocks**, **Log**, or **Share** of the ground there.
- Below, the picked ore's details: its biomes, its bands in numbers, **Add a band**, **Laid** to turn that ore on or off, and **Remove**. The band picked is ringed in the accent, as its candle is; the band whose candle is pointed at is lit, and pointing at a band lights its candle.
- **Delete** (Backspace on a Mac) takes the picked ore out; **⌘Z** (Ctrl+Z) puts back what was taken out, an ore or a band. Not while a number is typed.
- **Biomes**: **Only in** lays the ore only in the biomes ticked (none ticked: nowhere), **Except in** never in those ticked; **Add** opens the biomes, with a search, and a chip's × takes one off. The export, the cut and the amounts follow; the other ores stay where they were.
**Minecraft 1.18+** and **Before 1.18** start from the game's own; **Richness** multiplies every band; **Lay ores** turns them all off; **Large veins** adds the game's large veins: copper ore in granite between y 0 and 50, iron ore in tuff between y −60 and −8, with blocks of raw metal. Ores only take the place of stone, granite, diorite and andesite (deepslate and tuff get the deepslate ores), never of a block in the open. The settings are saved with the map; the World tab of the export can leave them out of one export.

# The window
## Menus
**Massif**, **File**, **Edit**, **View** and **Help** hold every command, each item with its shortcut beside it, greyed out when it cannot be used (what acts on the map while the home screen is shown, everything while a dialog is open).
- On a Mac they are in the menu bar at the top of the screen, as in every Mac app; the window's top bar keeps its buttons. **About Massif**, **Settings…** (`⌘,`) and **Quit Massif** (`⌘Q`) are in the **Massif** menu, and **Help** has the system's search of the menus.
- **Help ▸ Join the Discord…** opens Massif's community server in your browser: questions, maps to show, and **#bug-reports**.
- On Windows and Linux they are at the top left of the window, and the first, **Massif**, holds what the Mac's does: **About Massif**, **Settings…** (`Ctrl ,`), **Check for Updates…** (asks GitHub at once and shows the answer in Settings ▸ Updates), **Full Screen** (`F11`) and **Quit** (`Ctrl Q`).
- `⌘Q` (`Ctrl Q`) asks first when the map has changes that are not saved, and so does quitting from the Dock or the app switcher (`⌘Tab`, then `Q`): Massif comes to the front to ask. A logout, a restart or a shut-down waits for the answer: **Save** or **Don't Save**, and it goes on once Massif has quit; **Cancel**, and it stops.

## Areas
The window is made of areas, as in Blender. Each area shows one editor: the map, the 3D view, tools, settings, brushes, palettes, levels or information.
- The icon before an area's title opens the panel chooser: every editor on one page, in columns: **View** (Map, Diorama, 3D View, 3D Sculpt), **World** (Info, Versions), **Tools** (Tools, Tool Settings, Brushes, Custom Brushes, Brush Settings), **Paint** (Terrain, Layers, Packs, Blocks, Biomes, Levels), **Terrain** (Generator Graph, Generator Viewer, Node Library, Node Properties) and **Render** (Render Viewport, Sun and Sky, Camera, Render Output, Timeline). The line under them says what the one under the pointer is for, and whether another area shows it already. An empty area holds the same page.
- The **…** menu of an area splits it, joins it with its neighbour, maximises it or closes it.
- **Float** in that menu puts the area in a window of its own: move it anywhere on your screens, onto another one, and size it as any window. **Dock back** in its **…** menu puts it back, and so does closing its window. Floating panels are kept with their workspace and open where they were. (Where the system gives no windows of their own, the panel floats over the layout: drag its title to move it, its corner to size it, Shift-drag it onto an area to dock it there.)
- Drag any corner of an area into it to split it where you let go: the new area opens on that corner's side (across: left or right; up or down: above or below).
- Drag an area by its title onto another to move it there, onto its centre to swap them. Drag the gaps between areas to resize them.
- `Ctrl Space` maximises the area under the pointer, and gives it back its place.

## Workspaces
The tabs at the top are workspaces: arrangements of areas for a kind of work. The sides of Massif are switched at the top left, each remembering its workspace: **World**, the map editor (**Layout**, **Sculpting**, **Painting**, **3D**, **3D Sculpt**, **Maps**, **Minimal**, **WorldPainter**), **Terrain**, the terrain generator (**Generator**), and **Render** (Beta), where the map is path-traced and saved as images (**Still**, **Animation**; see Render).
- `+` adds a copy of the workspace shown; **View ▸ Reset workspaces** puts them all back as they came.
- Right-click a tab: **Reset** puts it back as it was at first, **Duplicate** copies it, **Delete** removes it.

## Moving around
The map moves under the hand: let go of a drag while moving and it glides on, slowing down; the zoom buttons, the zoom keys and **Fit the whole map** glide there in a moment. While a view moves, the others put off the work they do in the background, so that it keeps up, and pick it up as soon as it stops. A 3D view the workspace no longer shows stops building its tiles at once, and after 20 seconds out of sight gives back the memory it holds on the graphics card; shown again, it builds them again. Opened on a large map, it first shows all of it within a couple of seconds from what the 2D map has read, then every column, the nearest first. On a map too large for every block (drawn with a column every 2, 4 or 8 blocks), the 3D views draw every block, textured, around the eyes once they come low over the land: about 3 km around, ready a second or so after. **Settings ▸ Developer** shows the frames a second and the time of each, live.

## The 2D map
The buttons in the map's header pick what it shows (**Terrain**, **Height**, **Slope**, **Biome**, **Relief**, **True Colour**) and what goes over it: contour lines, **Block steps**, the grid of chunks, tiles and region files (the strong lines, 512 blocks apart, each region named in its corner as its file is, `r.1.-2.mca`; the footer names the region under the pointer too). The contour lines are WorldPainter's: a solid dark line every 10 blocks, finer ones halfway; the arrow beside their button (or a right-click on it) sets the **Interval** (5, 10, 20, 50 or any), their **Strength**, **Black** or **White**, **Crisp** or **Smooth**, and the fine lines. Zoomed out, they space out to 20, 50, 100… blocks so that they never run together. With **Block steps** on (the default), close up the map is shaded block by block, each at the height the game will give it and compared with its neighbour on the side the light comes from, as the game's own maps are: every step of a block shows as a sharp line, lit where it faces the light and in shadow at its foot, and the contour lines follow the blocks. Off, the shading follows the smooth shape of the ground. A little further out, where a pixel spans 2 × 2 or 4 × 4 blocks, the hillsides keep their smooth light and each step still draws its line over it, fainter the further out; from 8 blocks a pixel, the shading is the smooth one either way.

**True Colour** shows the map as it will look in the game once exported: each block's top in its texture's colour, grass, leaves, vines and water tinted by the biome under them (painted or automatic) from the game's colour maps, so a River biome has green grass, as in the game. Plants, trees and mods' blocks show in their own colours. The colours follow the blocks read in the **Blocks** panel: the game's, then each mod, then each resource pack added there in order, the last pack winning (right-click a pack's chip to read it earlier or later). As in the game, every pack goes over every mod, whatever the order they were added in: a pack that retextures or recolours a mod's blocks shows its own look. A pack's `colormap/grass.png` and `foliage.png` replace the game's, and OptiFine's custom colour maps (`optifine/colormap/*.properties`: `format=vanilla`, `grid` by biome and height, or `fixed`; `water.png`, `pine.png`, `birch.png`, `swampgrass.png`; `palette.block.*`) colour the blocks they name. A note at the bottom left of the map names whose colours it shows. The 3D views have the same mode, and the Render side tints its blocks with the same maps.
- When the workspace shows the 3D view too, a dot in the accent colour marks on the map where it is, the point it turns about (or the player, ringed), with a wedge the way it looks. It follows the 3D view as it moves; drag the dot to move the 3D view there. With the grid shown, a small faint plus marks the world's origin, x 0, z 0.

## Info
The **Info** panel shows what is under the cursor, on the map or in the 3D view, as WorldPainter's does: the coordinates, the height, the water and its depth, the terrain, the biome (painted, or the automatic one), whether it is selected, and every layer painted there — cover, trees, under the trees, tree size, pockets, smoothed steps, combined layers, annotation — each with its intensity where it has one. The world's name, seed, sea level, spawn, blocks, size and memory are in **File ▸ World Information…**.

## Messages
What Massif did, and what it does in the background (saving, exporting, automatic tools over the whole map), shows at the bottom right of the window. Long tasks have a **Cancel** button.

# Sculpting
## The tools
- **Raise / Lower**: left button raises, right button lowers.
- **Smooth**: rounds the land off.
- **Flatten**: levels the land to the height where the stroke starts. In Tool Settings, **Both ways** (the default), **Only raise** (fills what is under the height, leaves what is over it) or **Only lower** (the other way round); **Apply theme** repaints the land it flattens with the terrain its new height calls for.
- **Set height**: sets the land to a height. `Alt`-click on the map picks it.
- **Roughen**: adds or removes roughness.
- **Road**: click points on the map: a smooth road runs through them. Drag a point to move it, drag the road to add one, right-click or `Delete` to take one off; `Enter` builds it, `Esc` drops it. In Tool Settings: its **Width**, **Banks**, **Steepest slope** (it cuts and fills to climb no steeper), **Surface** (a path, gravel, cobblestone, stone bricks, a terrain or the Paint tool's noise) and **Bridges over water** (a deck a block over the water, on a level named Bridges; without them the road is built up out of the water). It keeps to the selection; the brush filters choose where its surface is painted. One step to undo.

## Brushes
A brush has a shape, a size, an intensity and a rotation. The shapes are the built-in brushes, terrain stamps (whole mountains, dunes, craters) and your own images.
- Scroll: size (`Shift`: one block at a time). `−` and `+` too.
- `1` … `9`, `0`: intensity 10 % … 100 %.
- `[` `]` or `Alt` + scroll: turn the brush, as in WorldPainter (`⌘` + scroll too).
- **Pencil** (Brush Settings): a hard line at full strength that follows the mouse block after block, without a gap however fast it moves, with any tool (terrain, layers, biomes, height). One block wide, its blocks join by their sides, never only by a corner; **Width** makes it wider.

**Your brushes** are sorted into groups, Massif's brush palettes: each group is a folder of Massif's brushes folder, saved there. A click on a group's title folds or opens it; right-click a group made in Massif to **Rename** or **Delete** it (its brushes move to Custom Brushes, none is deleted). Drag a brush onto another group, or right-click it ▸ **Move to group**, to move it there. WorldPainter's brushes folders are listed too, to paint with, but stay as they are.

## Where the brush acts
The options under the brush settings limit where a brush acts, as in WorldPainter:
- **Height limits**: only at or above a height, at or below one, between two or beyond them. The range is the map's own: from its bottom (−64 for a Minecraft map, or the bottom its file says) to its top. The picture shows the map from the side, cut from west to east through its highest point, with the sea in blue; the land at the heights the brush acts on, between the limits' lines, is in the accent. Drag a limit's line up or down (Shift: finely), or type the heights; the line under the settings says how much of the map the brush acts on.
- **What is on the map**: only on, or except on, some terrains, biomes, water or dry land, or layers (ground covers, trees and objects, under the trees, pockets, annotations). **Only on** asks for every kind listed at once, as WorldPainter's: a cover and a terrain means that cover on that terrain; two terrains, two biomes or two covers mean either. **Add** lists them to search, sorted **On the map** (what the map holds first), **Recent** or **A–Z**. The **eyedropper** picks on the map: click it, then a spot of the 2D map or the 3D view, and pick what is there in the list by the pointer; the brush is back in hand after, `Esc` puts it down.
- **Slope** and **Facing**: only on slopes steeper or gentler than an angle, or facing one way.

# Selections
## Selecting an area
The **Select** tools mark the part of the map the other tools change: once something is selected, every brush, a fill, the 3D tools on the map, **Auto biome**, **Auto paint**, noise painting and **Edit ▸ Whole map** act only inside it, and fade along its edge where it is feathered. A stroke all outside it does nothing, and a word at the foot of the window says so, once a stroke. A stroke that leaves part of the map looking as it was says why too, once it ends: a ground cover of blocks lying over the terrain painted (a WorldPainter map's custom ground covers of sand or gravel come in as such: the terrain is painted under them; rub the cover out with **Ground cover**'s right button, or hide it to see the terrain), a level over the one painted, a locked layer, the brush's filter, or a layer hidden on the map. The Info panel says it of the column under the cursor, for the tool in hand. A dashed outline, its dashes marching (still with **Reduce motion**), shows it on the map.
- **Rectangle** and **Ellipse**: drag across the map. **Lasso**: draw around an area; let go and it closes. **Selection brush**: paint it with the brush (right button: take it out); its soft edge feathers it.
- Shift or ⌘ (Ctrl) adds to the selection, Alt or the right button takes out of it, both together keep only what both hold; the **New · Add · Subtract · Intersect** buttons set what a shape does with no key held. A right-click, **Esc** or **Clear** beside the modes clears the selection (a left click without dragging too, in the New mode).
- **Feather** fades new shapes over that many blocks on either side of their edge (up to 64); **Feather it** softens the selection there is.
- **Select by** picks the columns of the whole map with a terrain (the one picked in Terrain or Blocks), between two heights, or on slopes steeper or gentler than an angle, as the brush filters read them.
- **All**, **Invert** and **Deselect** are in the tool settings and in the Edit menu (⌘A, ⇧⌘I, ⌘D).

Each change of the selection is a step to undo. The selection is saved in the map's file (versions before 0.6 open the file without it). **Export** can keep to it: **Only the selection** writes only the tiles of 128 × 128 blocks it reaches, to try a part of the map in the game.

# Painting
## Terrains and biomes
**Paint** gives the ground a terrain: pick it in the Terrain palette. To blend two terrains into each other instead of meeting on a line, take the **Gradient brush**: beside Paint in the Paint row of the Tools panel (a swatch fading out, with a dot until you try it), or **Gradient** among the ways to paint at the top of Paint terrain's Tool Settings (**One terrain**, **Noise**, **Gradient**). **Biome** paints biomes the same way: pick one in the Biomes palette. The right button takes a biome back to the automatic one, and a terrain back to the map's own: the terrain a new map was filled with (one covering nearly all of an older map), shown under **Right button** in Tool Settings, where a click on it chooses another. The terrain Paint lays shows at the top of its Tool Settings: click it to search the terrains and blocks, the ones picked lately first. On a map whose terrains came from its height, or with **By height**, the right button puts back what the map's **Zones** call for; **Zones** among the ways to paint lays them with the left button. The zones paint by height, slope and water, read from the top; start from a preset (Classic, Plains, Mountain, Mesa, Desert, Taiga, Coast), or **Edit these zones** to make your own, saved with the map or under a name in the settings. Grass, snow and the like are never painted under water: sand, clay, dirt and gravel by depth instead.

The **Terrain** panel lists the map's terrains by category, each folding at a click on its title. Drag a terrain onto another category, or right-click it ▸ **Move to** (or **New category…**), to sort it; right-click a category to rename it, move it, hide its terrains or delete it (its terrains go to Other). **Hide from the list** takes a terrain out of the lists, not off the map: it is under **Hidden**, at the end. The map keeps its categories.

**The Eyedropper tool** (⌥Y, in the Navigate row of the Tools panel) works as WorldPainter's: click the 2D map or a 3D view, and what is there to paint with is listed by the pointer: its terrain, its biome (painted, or the one the land calls for) and every layer painted there (ground cover, trees, under the trees, tree size, pockets, annotation, combined layer). Click one to paint with it: its tool is chosen with it ready (Paint terrain, Paint biome, the layer's tool). Where there is only a terrain, it is picked at once. **Shift-click** takes the terrain, height and water there at once and goes back to the tool you had. `Esc`, or a click elsewhere, puts the list away. Alt-click while painting still picks what the tool in hand paints.

**The Spawn point tool** (in the Navigate row of the Tools panel) sets where players appear, as WorldPainter's Spawn tool: click the 2D map or a 3D view and the spawn point goes on the ground there (on the water, if there is some), as one step to undo. A pin marks it on the map and in the 3D views; drag it on the map to move it. With Pan or this tool, a right-click on the map offers **Set spawn here**. **File ▸ World Information…** shows and sets its numbers.

**The Measure tool** (in the Navigate row of the Tools panel) measures the map, a block being a metre. With **Line**, drag across the map, or click from point to point and double-click (or Enter) to end: the Info panel gives its distance on the map and along the ground, its heights, its mean and steepest slopes (% and degrees) and the profile of the land under it (point at the profile: the map marks the spot). With **Area**, drag around a part of the map or click its corners and the first one again: the Info panel gives its area, perimeter, lowest, highest and mean heights, the blocks over and under a level (its lowest ground, the sea level or a height), the share under water and its terrains, biomes and layers with their shares. It works in the 3D views too (click points on the land). A measure is never saved: it shows while the tool is in hand, or with **Keep on screen** with the other tools too; `Esc`, a right-click or **Clear** clears it. A line's label names its values ("21.06 km · ↓54 blocks · slope 10 %": distance, height from start to end, mean slope); point at it, or look in the Info panel, for its **travel time** walking, sprinting, sprint-jumping, on a fast horse, by boat on water or ice, by minecart and with an elytra and rockets, with the Minecraft days (20 minutes each) once past one. **The Travel range tool** (Navigate row) draws rings around the pointer, on the map and on the land in 3D: how far players get in a time, each ring a time in minutes or MC days and a way of going (Tool Settings; one and two days on foot at first, up to eight rings), labelled with its time and radius. Distances are straight and flat, the farthest one gets. A click pins the rings; `Esc`, a right-click or **Unpin** lets them go. The **Info** panel shows the column under the cursor in four parts: **Position** (coordinates, chunk, region file, levels, selection), **Ground** (height, water, terrain, biome), **Layers here** and **Measure**.

Under the map's terrains, **From your game and mods** lists the blocks that make ground (no glass, lamps, furniture, doors, slabs or plants), by source and category, folded; click one to paint with it, and it becomes a terrain of the map in its category. **Every block** shows the others, and a search finds any block. Once the mods are read, **Sort … Imported terrains by mod and kind** sorts a WorldPainter world's Imported terrains into their blocks' categories in one click. A world made of a mod's blocks says so in its import report, which offers to read the mod first.

**Edit ▸ Replace terrain…** paints every column of one terrain with another, over the map or in the selection, in one step to undo: to merge two terrains of the same block, such as a WorldPainter world's Imported one and a mod's.

## Block palettes
A block palette says what the terrains are made of: the grass, rock, sand, gravel and snow the generator lays by height and slope, the other natural terrains, and the rock under them where caves are carved and ores laid. A terrain a palette leaves out is the game's; terrains of your own are never touched.
- **Vanilla** comes with Massif. A mod may bring a palette of its blocks (Conquest Reforged's mixes its grass blocks, loamy dirt, limestones, sands, gravels and snow in patches), offered only once that mod's blocks are read: the menus list Vanilla, the palettes of the mods read, and your own.
- **Blocks** in the New map settings picks the palette of a new map. **Blocks** in the Info panel makes the whole map of another palette at once, in one step to undo.
- **Edit palettes…** at the bottom of either menu: **Duplicate** a palette or make one **From this map**, pick a terrain, then for each layer its depth, **In patches**, and its blocks with their weights. Click a layer, then a block in the grid, or type any id (`mod:block[axis=x]`). **Rock under the terrains** sets the rock above and under Y 0. Your palettes are kept with the settings.

## Blocks from mods

**Terrain** and **Blocks** are two panels for two things: **Terrain** lists the map's terrains (what each part of the ground is made of, its top block and the layers under it); **Blocks** lists every block of your game and its mods, to paint with (a whole block becomes a terrain, a plant a ground cover). Which game and mods those blocks come from is set in **File ▸ Game & mods…**, also opened by the button at the top of the Blocks panel: the version of Minecraft, the modpack instances found here (each with **Use**), **Instance…**, **Add mods…** and the resource packs. Massif finds the game's jar beside each instance and wherever the launchers keep it (the official launcher, CurseForge on any drive, the Modrinth App, Prism, MultiMC, ATLauncher, GDLauncher).

- Massif finds the instances installed here with mods with blocks, whatever the mods, and lists those not read yet in the Blocks panel under **Mods with blocks in your Minecraft instances**: a row each, with its loader, version of the game and number of mods with blocks. Click a row to unfold its mods, one a line by name with its id and number of blocks (its jar's file name on hover); the search above the list finds instances and mods by name, id or file. **Use** reads one in one step, its mods, their lists of blocks for WorldPainter (`<mod>_wp.csv`) and its resource packs, over the version of the game it plays.
- **Game & mods… ▸ Mods ▸ Instance…** reads any modpack's instance the same way, with the blocks its KubeJS scripts make (those its startup scripts register, with their names, shapes and textures, and those of its `kubejs/assets` folder); connected textures made for Fusion or OptiFine (`optifine/ctm`, read by Continuity too) show one tile at the size of a block, and join their neighbours in the True Colour view; **Add mods…** takes jars of mods with blocks (Conquest Reforged, Chipped, Macaw's…), resource packs (`.zip`) and lists of blocks made for WorldPainter (`.csv`) one by one. Click a chip to take it out.
- A mod's blocks show in its own creative tabs when it gives them, with their textures, else by family (a block's family read from its name, or from how it is drawn: plants, wood, building blocks). A plant drawn with a model of its own (duckweed, clover, leaf litter, ivy) is told by the game's tags of plants the mod puts it in. Whole blocks, plants and the ground's blocks (paths, farmland) show at first; **All shapes** adds each block's slabs, stairs, arches and other shapes, and the search looks through all of them.
- **Any block by its id**: type it under the search, with its properties (`mod:block[axis=x]`), then **As terrain** or **As cover**. A block the panel does not have is written as typed, in grey on the map. A whole block paints as a terrain, any other as a ground cover; tall plants grow in two halves, and plants grow on the mod's own grass, moss and soils.
- Massif comes with palettes for Conquest Reforged, Biomes O' Plenty, Regions Unexplored and Oh The Biomes We've Gone, offered once their blocks are read. A palette for another mod is a data file anyone can write: see the guide's *Mod profiles*.
- **Biomes of mods**: once a mod is read, the **Biomes** panel lists the biomes the game, its mods and its data packs define (the instance's `datapacks` and `global_packs` too), under each mod's name, in their colours; the 3D view tints grass and leaves as each biome does. Type `namespace:biome` in its search and **Use** it for a biome of a mod not read here. Right-click a biome of the map that is not the game's (a mod's, a data pack's, one typed or brought from WorldPainter) to set its **Name**, its **Map colour** and the colours of its **Grass** and **Leaves** (the 3D view and True Colour tint them so); its id stays, as it is exported. Auto biome's **Rules…** offer them for each kind of land, and a mod's profile can give a terrain of its block a biome. The export writes them by their ids: the game shows them with the mod installed.
- **Export** picks the version the mod is made for and its instance's saves, and warns in red when the version or the mod does not match. Blocks are written by their own ids and states: open the world with the same mods in the game.

## Automatic biomes and paint
- **Auto biome** reads the land and gives it biomes: water, rivers and beaches, lowland, hills, mountains, peaks, cliffs, from its water, height, slope and ground. Sand at the water's edge is a beach; sand far from the water is a desert only where sand is all around, not a patch in a plain.
- **Auto paint** paints the ground as its biome calls for: a terrain for its ground, one for its cliffs, one under its water. Its **Style** (top of Tool Settings) is **Follow biome** by default; **Temperate forest**, **Jungle**, **Mountain**, **Desert**, **Badlands**, **Taiga**, **Snowy**, **Savanna**, **Coast** and **Swamp** paint by the shape of the land alone, whatever the biomes (a desert painted as a jungle): a terrain for the shore, the ground, the hills, the mountains, the peaks, the cliffs and under water. **Custom** sets them one by one; **Save as preset** keeps it under your name for it, **Delete** takes it out, **Change…** makes a custom style of the one shown.
A column keeps the biome it has when its ground agrees with it: a desert on sand (as the Terrain Generator's climate paints it), badlands on red sand, a savanna, taiga or jungle on grass, snowy biomes on snow. A map filled with rock is read from the shape of its land; one mostly of sand is a desert. Auto biome's Tool Settings list what the preview's colours stand for.

Hovered over the map, they show what the brush would change, in its new colour; what already has what the land calls for (painted by an earlier stroke, say) is hatched, and a bar under the map names what the columns become and how much stays as it is. Biomes show on the map in its **Biome** mode.

Both work with the brush, or over the whole map at once: **Whole map** in the tool settings, or **Edit ▸ Auto biome: whole map** and **Auto paint: whole map**. Over the whole map they run in the background, with their progress, and are one step to undo. **Rules…** sets the heights and slopes the land is read with, the biome of each kind of land and the terrains of each biome.

Hovered over the map, the brush shows in colour what would change.

## The whole map at once
**Edit ▸ Whole map** runs one of the terrain graph's filters over the whole map: **Erosion** (rain carries soil from the heights to the valleys), **Thermal erosion** (loose rock slides where a slope is too steep to hold it), **Rivers** (traced where the water of the map runs, dug into the valleys and shaped as the River tool's: falling from pool to pool down steep valleys, wide over flat beds on gentle ground, their banks a valley, their channels going on under the sea; gravel under their water, sand in the shallows; the narrowest a quarter of **Width**; their water held block by block, with no dam of land across them), **Lakes** (water fills the hollows up to where they spill, each lake at or under its rim, no wider than a 64th of the land, its floor dug into a bowl, sand in its shallows and on its flat shores), **Smooth**, **Sharpen**, **Terraces**, **Strata**, and **Scale and raise** (the relief scaled around the sea level, then moved up or down). Each opens with its settings — those of its node in the graph — and the map small, before and after, made again as they change. **Apply to the whole map** runs it in the background, with its progress and a **Cancel**; it is one step to undo. The ground's paint, its levels and what was sculpted in 3D stay as they are; maps over 4096 blocks across are worked out a sample every few blocks, the change spread over the blocks between, so they keep their detail.

## Caves
Caves are a layer painted on the map, carved as the game carves them since 1.18: **Chambers** (large rooms with pillars), **Tunnels** (long and winding), **Noodles** (thin) and **Ravines**, each with its **Density**, **Size** and heights, a **Roof** of rock under the ground except where they **open to the sky** (on dry land only), and **Aquifers**: water, and lava deep down, at levels of their own, walled off from each other by rock, never running out. The **Caves** tool (Underground group) paints a layer where its caves go, the right button rubs it out; its settings change the caves wherever it is painted, at any time. **Edit ▸ Whole map ▸ Caves…** paints a layer over the map or the selection in one step to undo, after a picture of its caves: a plan at a height you choose and a cut through the land. **Underground** on the 2D map cuts it through at a height; **X-ray** in the 3D views shows the caves through the ground. With the new terrain generator, the graph makes caves too: its **Cave layer**, **Cave biome** and **Cave System** nodes give the map a cave layer of its own and cave biomes, and its volumes (a tube along a line, a hall, cave noise) are carved out of the ground or added to it when the graph is applied.

## Layer by layer
The **Layer** tool (Underground group) edits the map block by block, a layer at a time. In hand, it cuts the 2D map at a height — the **Underground** button and its height in the map's header, or **PageUp** and **PageDown** a layer at a time (**Shift**: 8) — and the map shows that layer: rock grey (the soil's colour near its top, a block's own colour where one was filled), caves dark, water blue, lava orange, the land over the cut faded. The brush then **Dig**s, **Fill**s with a block (the column's rock, or the Terrain panel's terrain), pours **Water** or **Lava**, or paints a cave **Biome** on that layer, or on **Layers under** and **Layers over** it too: **Round** makes each dab a ball, so a round brush digs a ball. The right button fills back with rock. Each stroke is one step to undo. Caves made by a terrain graph show on the layer as painted ones do, and what you change here stays when the graph is applied again.

## Cave biomes
The **Cave biome** tool paints **Lush caves**, **Dripstone caves** or the **Deep dark** between two heights, by cells of 4 blocks as the game keeps them. The export writes the game's cave biomes and decorates their caves: moss, azaleas, vines with glow berries, dripleaves by clay pools; dripstone blocks, stalactites and stalagmites; sculk, veins, sensors, shriekers and catalysts. Other caves get some glow lichen.

## Import mask
**Edit ▸ Import mask…** paints an image onto the map, as WorldPainter's Import mask: PNG (8 or 16 bits, grey, colour, alpha), TIFF (integers or floats), BMP, JPEG or OpenEXR (as greys, black and white of a height starting at its lowest and highest values). **What it paints**: the **Annotations** (each pixel's nearest of the 15 colours, or one colour), a **Layer** (a ground cover, trees, under the trees, pockets, a tree size), a **Terrain**, a **Biome**, **Water** up to a height, or the **Height** (black and white give the heights set: offset and scale). **Where it lands**: at the world's origin or fitted to the map, then a **Scale** (about its middle, 100 % a block a pixel) and an **Offset** (a block a step, across the map); the map small shows where it lands: drag the image on it, or move it with the arrow keys, a block a press (Shift: 16). **How its values are read**: on from a **threshold**, or a **strength** (a share of the columns, scattered); the range from black to white or the image's own; **Invert**; **Black leaves the map as it is**. **With what is there**: **Replace** (the image decides, rubbing out where it is off), **Add**, **Max** or **Min**. Locked layers keep their columns, the selection is kept to, and **Import** runs in the background as one step to undo; large images stream through, never whole in memory. **Keep the image as a reference image** (on to start with) leaves it on the 2D map as a reference image exactly where it lands, at an opacity, to see the source under what it painted: kept with the map, never exported, shown, moved or removed with the map's Reference images button. **Several images** go in at once (pick them together, or **Add images…**): each has its tab and target, all land in the same place, and they are one step to undo, the heights first. **Blend the heights' edges over** fades a heightmap into the land around it over that many blocks, without a cliff at the seam. Where the window has room, it opens as the **mask window**: the map large with the image over it, seen **Over the map**, as the **Mask** alone, **Before**, **After**, **Before | After** (drag the line) or **Changes**, at an **Overlay** opacity; the wheel zooms, the right button pans, the left one drags the image. **What each value paints** is its legend; **Levels** shows the histogram with **Black point**, **White point**, **Midtones** and **Blur**, applied to the import; under the map, how many columns change. **Smaller** goes back to the dialog. **Several images** go in at once (pick them together, or **Add images…**): each has its tab and target, all land in the same place, and they are one step to undo, the heights first. **Blend the heights' edges over** fades a heightmap into the land around it over that many blocks, without a cliff at the seam.

## Export Region
**File ▸ Export Region…** writes a part of the map (the selection, the whole map, or blocks typed in) as images of 1 pixel a block into a folder of its own: `height.png` (16 bits, black the world's floor, white its top), an image for each terrain and layer found there (white where it is), and on demand each biome, the water and the annotations, with a `region.txt` saying where the region lies. Work on them in another tool, then **Edit ▸ Import Mask…** with the images: the `region.txt` beside them puts them back in place (**Where region.txt says**) and their names say what each paints. With **Review it when it comes back**, a version is kept and `zone.json` written: the region comes back as a contribution to review (see Working together).

## Selection as schematic
**File ▸ Export Selection as Schematic…** (or **Export as Schematic…** under the selection in a selection tool's settings) writes the selected columns as a schematic with the blocks the export to Minecraft writes there: ground, water, caves, plants, trees and structures with their chests. **WorldEdit .schem** (Sponge version 3), **WorldEdit 7.2 .schem** (version 2) or **Litematica**; down to a depth **under the lowest ground** or **the world's floor**, up to the highest block; trees, structures and biomes on or off; **Keep the air** (off: structure voids, which Litematica leaves as they are); the origin at the middle of its foot or its corner. Before it is saved, the dialog shows its size, its blocks, the file's size, the **materials** it holds and the trees of each object layer; **Copy as text** copies them. In an object layer's list, **Export as .schem…** writes the objects picked, a file each.

## Noise painting
**Noise** (in the Paint tool's settings) paints with several terrains or blocks at once, mixed by a noise, as Axiom's noise painter does: patches, veins, blobs, cells or stripes, of simplex, Perlin or value noise, with its size, detail (octaves, lacunarity, gain), stretch and direction. The blocks are a list in order — neighbours meet on the ground — each with its share of the ground; drag one to move it, click its name to change it, **Add a block** to add one. Presets are a start, and a pattern can be saved under a name. Hovered over the map, the brush shows what it would paint; **Paint the whole map** lays it everywhere the brush options let it, in the background. **Blend** dithers neighbours in the list into each other across their shares: at 100 % the list is an ordered gradient (dirt → coarse dirt → grass) with no hard line. The same mix is the graph's **Noise paint** node, with the same presets: saved from the brush or the node, they show in both.

## Snowfall
**Edit ▸ Whole map ▸ Snowfall…** lays snow by a small simulation: **Intensity** (blocks of snow fallen), the band of heights it falls on (**Lowest**, **Highest**, **Fade**), **Deepest** (in snow layers, 8 a block), **Accumulation** (how much settles where it falls, the rest drifts), **Steepest slope** (steeper slopes shed it), the **Wind**'s **Strength** and where it blows **From** (it strips the slopes facing it and piles drifts in the lee of ridges and in hollows), **Iterations**, and **Only on Frost and snow covers**. A picture shows the snow before it is laid. **Let it snow** raises each column by its snow in eighths of a block — snow layers 1 to 8, then snow blocks —, in the Snow terrain, smoothed so that the export writes the layers; one step to undo, within the selection.


## Reference images
A picture over or under the 2D map to trace over, never exported: **Edit ▸ Add Reference Image…**, or drop a picture (PNG, JPEG, WebP, BMP, TIFF or GIF) on the map. The picture button in the map's header lists them with an eye and a lock; click one for **Over the map** or **Under it**, **Opacity**, **Middle**, **Scale** (blocks a pixel) and **Rotation**. The images are passive: clicks and strokes go to the tool in hand. With the **Pan** tool, or after **Edit placement** (until **Done** or `Esc`), drag one to move it, a corner to scale it, the knob above it to turn it (Shift: by 15°). They are saved with the map.

## Gradient brush
**Gradient brush** (in the Paint tools) blends two terrains over their edge instead of a hard line: paint the base terrains first, then drag it over the boundary — rock, then a rim, then grass. A gradient is terrains in **bands** of so many blocks, A → B → C, with a **transition** between each two: a **Hard cut**, **Noise** (a ragged edge; its intensity and scale) or **Blobs** (of every size, denser and denser; their density and size), and a **midpoint** where the two are half and half. A band can be any terrain, or any block of the game or of its mods.
![gradient]
**Across an edge**, each column takes the band its distance to the boundary says: the bands before the **edge** lie on the side of the first terrain, the others on the side of the last; past the gradient's ends the ground stays as it is. The boundary is the one between the gradient's first and last terrains, and only those two are painted: a terrain it does not paint (a beach) and the ground under water stay as they are, and clicking again over the same edge paints the same thing. **Wander** widens and narrows the bands along the edge. In a selection it runs in from the selection's edge. **From the centre** lays it from the brush's centre to its rim: a patch with rings. The right button paints it the other way round. It keeps to the selection, filters, intensity and soft edges, one step to undo, the same ground for the same stroke.
**Edit…** opens its editor: **Presets** to start from; drag the stops under the bar to move blocks between neighbours, click between them to add one, drag a diamond to move a midpoint and the marker over the bar to move the edge; click a band or a transition to change it. **Save** keeps it among your gradients and in the map; **Export…** and **Import…** share it as a `.massifpack`, and **File ▸ Export Pack…** offers the map's gradients.

# Decor
## Ground covers
**Ground cover** paints what grows over the ground: grass, tall grass, flowers, ferns, mushrooms, snow, seagrass, or any blocks of your own, with their density and how high they grow. On the map a cover shows in its colour, at the **Opacity** set beside it: in full where something grows, lighter where the ground stays bare, so the denser it grows the more it shows. **Frost** and **Lava** come with every new map, as in WorldPainter: Frost lays snow and turns the top of the water to ice, Lava turns the water into lava. Any cover can do either: **The water** in its settings (As it is, Frozen, Lava). Older maps find them in Packs.

## Layers
How every layer fits together (kinds, the slots of a block, masks, the lock) is in the guide's Layers page. A ground cover's blocks (**Grows**, in its settings) are edited with their states, as WorldPainter's block picker: click a row (or add a block) and each property of its block shows under the list with the values it allows, read from the game's and the mods' files — `layers`, `facing`, `half`, `waterlogged`, `age`… —, **Default** for the game's own, the state it makes written under them; **Random** beside `facing`, `rotation` or `age` grows it every way, one row for each value. The **Layers** panel (under Terrain; or any area's chooser, in Paint) lists everything painted over the ground, as WorldPainter's layers: the map's ground covers, its object layers and the terrain mixes saved for noise painting. Click one to paint with it (its settings open in Tool Settings), double-click to rename it, click its eye to hide it on the map and in the 3D view (the export writes it all the same), and right-click for Settings… (a dialog of their own), **Find** (the map goes to the nearest place it is painted, and the 3D view with it when it follows; again, to the next place), Duplicate, Move up and down, Lock and Remove. The panel lists the combined layers first, then the others by the slot of a block they paint: terrain mixes, ground covers, trees and objects, under the trees, underground pockets, then the tree sizes and the annotations. A **locked** ground cover, tree layer or pocket layer keeps its blocks: no brush paints another layer of its slot over them, nor rubs them out, until it is unlocked (the lock shows on the right of a row pointed at). **New combined**, **New cover**, **Import objects…**, **New pockets** and **New mix** make new ones. At the bottom, the fifteen colours of the annotations: a click paints with one.

**Palettes**, at the top of the panel, sort your layers as WorldPainter's palettes do: named groups of layers of any kind (forests, rivers, a modpack's trees). **New palette** makes one and names it; drag a layer onto it, or right-click the layer ▸ **Palette**, to put it there; a layer in a palette is listed in it rather than in its section, and dragged onto a section's title (or ▸ Palette ▸ **Back to its section**) it goes back. Drag a layer onto another of its kind to put it above or below it (a line shows where), in a palette or a section, as one step to undo; dropped on a layer of another palette, it joins that palette there. The order only changes the list, never the export. A click on a palette's title folds or opens it, a double-click renames it, and its menu moves it up or down or deletes it (its layers go back to their sections; none is removed). A palette's **eye** hides or shows all its layers at once, ground covers and trees alike, on the map and in the 3D views; half an eye means some are hidden, and a click shows them all. **Alt-click** the eye (or ▸ **Show only this palette**) to show that palette alone, as WorldPainter's solo; again, the layers come back as they were. A single layer has its solo too: **Alt-click** its eye (or ▸ **Solo** in its menu) to draw only that cover or object layer on the map and in 3D (a combined layer: its cover and trees), the terrain still drawn; **Solo: its name** shows in the map's header, and a click there, Alt-click again or ▸ **Show all layers** shows every layer exactly as before. The map saves its palettes with its layers; a saved terrain mix keeps its palette with the mix. A WorldPainter world or layer file brings its custom layers in their palettes, hidden or soloed as they were.

The **Terrain** panel has its own solo. **Solo** shows only the ground, as WorldPainter's: every ground cover and object layer is hidden on the map and in 3D, **Solo: Terrain only** shows in the map's header, and unticking it (or a click on that chip) shows every layer as before. **Isolate this terrain** fades every terrain but the one in hand, to see at a glance where it is painted.

## Base layers, sub-canopy and tree sizes
A block has one plant layer and one tree layer: painting another over it takes its place. A tree layer can be a **sub-canopy** layer instead (**Canopy ▸ Sub-canopy** in its settings): it is painted beside the trees, not in their place, and its objects (hazel, holly, bushes) grow under their canopy, in the air the trees leave. The Bushes and Azalea bushes of Packs are sub-canopy layers. **Tree sizes** (Decor group, and its swatches in the Layers panel) marks where bushes, small, medium or large trees grow: paint the sizes, then one species over everything, and each spot takes its objects of the size marked. Its Tool Settings list the **sizes of this map**: rename and recolour them, add sizes of your own (**New size**: Tiny, Forest, Open-grown…), remove your own. Each object's size is set in its layer's object list (**Tree size**), or **Auto**: guessed from its height, a quarter of the layer's Auto objects each in Bushes, Small, Medium and Large. An object layer is spaced **By canopy** or **On a grid** (its settings). By canopy, two trees stand as far apart as their crowns' radii added, so that crowns touch without overlapping at full density, narrow and wide trees mixed, and never closer than its **Least gap** between trunks; each object's crown is measured from its leaves, or set by hand (**Canopy** in the object list). On a grid, one object at most stands in each square of the layer's spacing, whatever its size, as in WorldPainter. **Density of each size**, in the layer's settings, thins its bushes, small, medium, large trees or sizes of your own apart. Every kind of tree of the packs comes in the four sizes (its smaller ones, of weight 0, stand only where their size is painted). How every layer works together: see Layers in the guide.

## Combined layers
**Combined layer** (Decor group, and the first section of the Layers panel) paints several layers at once, as WorldPainter's combined layers: a forest's terrain, biome, cover and trees in one stroke. Its settings name the layer of each slot it paints (Terrain, Biome, Cover, Trees, Sub-canopy, Tree size, Pockets, or None) and the share of the columns painted each takes, the same columns every time. Each goes to its slot as its own tool paints it, so a block keeps one layer of each slot, and changing a combined layer changes what it paints next, not what it painted. The right button rubs out what it paints, the terrain put back as Paint terrain's right button does. A `.world`'s combined layers come in as combined layers.

## Underground pockets
**Underground pockets** (Decor group) lays blobs of other blocks in the rock under the ground, as WorldPainter's layer of that name: gravel, clay, granite, a mod's stone. A pocket layer has its blocks (with weights), its **share** of the rock where it is painted, the **size** of a pocket and the heights it keeps to; the pockets never reach into the ground's strata, caves or water. **New pockets** makes one. They show hatched over the map while the tool is in hand; the right button rubs them out. Ores grow in pockets of rock (andesite, granite, diorite, tuff) as in the rest of it, and the survival check counts the pockets' blocks.

## Smooth terrain
**Edit ▸ Smooth terrain…** builds the top of the land in partial blocks when the map is exported, following its precise height: **layers** (eighths, as snow's), **slabs**, **stairs** facing each one-block step (inner and outer ones in the corners), **vertical slabs** at the foot of cliffs and **corner** pieces; on slopes steeper than a block a block, the foot of each step is filled as at a one-block step (layers or a stair) where vertical slabs are not allowed. One switch turns it on for the whole map; each column takes the pieces of the block painted there, so repainting a terrain changes them with it. The page lists the map's terrains with the pieces each may use, greyed where its block has none; the defaults come from the game and the mods' profiles (Conquest Reforged's: layers for soils and sands, the whole range for rock). The pieces of a block are found by name and by the mods' stonecutter recipes, which also find shapes a mod names otherwise (Conquest's `limestone_slab` is stone's); a slab by eighths serves where layers are asked for. Pieces go only in the air or still water (waterlogged), never under an object. Under a cover's plants the land keeps its height in layers or slabs when the plants can sit on them: Conquest Reforged's plants (which have a `layers` property) are written as high as the layers under them, wherever they grow on layers, with nothing to set up; the game's plants, which would float, keep a whole block under them, and do not grow on a block of layers. The **Smoothing** brush (Decor group) turns it **On** or **Off** in places, or lays **A slab** of your choice; the right button gives the map's setting back. The 3D View shows the pieces.

## Annotations
**Annotations** (Paint group) marks the map in colour to plan it, as WorldPainter's annotations: where a town goes, a road, a river to dig. They show on the map in every mode, whatever the level, and on the land in 3D, and are never exported. The right button rubs them out.

- **Colours**: WorldPainter's fifteen, or any other with the **Colour** button of the tool's settings: from one of the fifteen it adds a colour of the map's own (kept with the map), from one of the map's own it changes it, marks and all.
- **Opacity**: each colour has its own, in the tool's settings.
- **Annotation layers**: **+** beside Annotations in the Layers panel makes one, with its own name and colour, as WorldPainter's custom annotation layers (which a `.world` brings in as such). Each is a layer of its own: the fifteen colours take each other's place on a block, but annotation layers overlap, on the colours and on each other, so that several mark the same blocks (biomes, tree sizes, flow lines, as masks). The brush and curves paint one over what is there, and the right button rubs out that one alone. Each is listed by name under the colours, or in its palette: a click paints with it, a double-click renames it, its eye shows or hides it alone (its marks are kept), and its menu sets its colour and opacity, finds it on the map, shows it alone (Solo), sorts it into a palette, as the other layers, or removes it with its marks (one step to undo). One in a palette is listed there only, not among the colours, and follows its palette's eye even with all the annotations hidden. As in WorldPainter, the layer you start painting with (a cover, trees, an annotation) is shown, whichever eye hid it. The Info panel lists every annotation of the block; a filter's **Only on** finds each layer wherever it is, under the others too. A WorldPainter layer set not to be exported (a placeholder marking where something goes) imports as an annotation layer of its name, marked where it was painted.
- **Show or hide them all**, on the map and in 3D: the eye in the tool's settings (and beside the colours in the Layers panel), the pen button in the map's header, or **View ▸ Show Annotations**.
- **Curves**: **Curve**, in place of **Brush**, draws a curve through points, as Axiom's Path tool: click to place points, drag a point to move it, drag the curve to add one, right-click (or Delete) to take one off. **Smooth** runs through every point, **Bézier** bends with the handles of the point picked, **Straight** goes from point to point. Shown as the ribbon it paints, from its **Start width** to its **End width**; **Paint curve** (Enter) paints it as an annotation in one step to undo, Escape drops it.

## Packs
**Packs** (the second tab of the Layers panel, or an area of its own) holds ready-made things to paint, as WorldPainter Modernised's Packs panel did: sixteen kinds of trees of the tree pack (tall and slender spruces, firs, larches, giant redwoods, big and pale oaks, birches, cherries, acacias, willows, rainforest giants, palms, dead trees, bushes, azaleas), each in eight shapes; presets of flowers, plants and water plants; and mixes of blocks for grass, soil, stone, sand, cliffs in beds, snow and volcanic rock. A click adds one to the map — an object layer, a ground cover or a terrain — and picks it to paint; a dot marks those the map has already. A search field filters them.

### Sharing packs
**File ▸ Export Pack…** makes a `.massifpack` file of the map's layers, to share: tick the object layers, ground covers and terrain mixes it holds, and give it a name, an author, a description and a picture (drawn from what it holds unless you choose one). **File ▸ Import Pack…**, the **Import…** button of the Packs panel, a file dropped on the window, or a double-click on it in the Finder or Explorer adds a pack to **Your packs**, at the top of the Packs panel, with its picture and author; its things paint like Massif's own. A name you have already asks to replace that pack or keep both under another name. The **⋯** menu of a pack saves a copy of it, or removes it from your packs (maps keep the layers they took from it). A pack holds blocks and settings only, never anything that runs; Massif checks every part of it and refuses a damaged, oversized or foreign file.

## Custom objects
**Objects** places trees, rocks or ruins from `.schem`, `.schematic`, `.bp`, `.litematic` and `.nbt` files: import them, or drop them on the window, then paint where they stand, how far apart and how many.

**Grow trees…** (in the Objects tool's settings, and beside **Add objects…** in an object layer's) grows trees from a few settings instead of files. Pick a kind to start from (Oak, Spruce, Birch, Cherry, Palm, Willow, Acacia, Dead tree, and two giants: Giant sequoia, about a hundred blocks tall, and Great oak), then change its **Height** (up to 128 blocks), **Trunk width**, how many **Limbs** leave the trunk, how much they **Droop** under their weight and **Reach for the sun**, the **Crown** (Cone, Round, Umbrella, Weeping, Palm), the size and **Fullness** of the clumps of leaves, and the **Wood** and **Leaves** blocks. **More settings** has the trunk's **Flare** at its foot, how far up the trunk goes, where the first limb leaves it, up to four **Branch levels**, their **Angle**, length and how **Crooked** they grow. The trunk flares into roots, smoothed with stairs and slabs; thick wood is logs laid along its axis, thinner branches walls (of the stone closest to the bark), then fences into the leaves, which leave gaps the wood shows through; and every leaf is persistent and within reach of wood. **Survival friendly** keeps to logs and leaves, the blocks a tree of the game has. The picture shows the tree of the **Seed**, and the next four seeds' under it (a click takes one); each seed grows another tree. **Add … trees to a new layer** makes an object layer of as many variants as **Trees** says, each of the next seed; **Add to** puts them in the layer picked. Smaller trees for Tree sizes (bushes and small trees) are grown with them, as for the packs' trees. A tree too tall to stand under the top of the world where it is painted (a giant on a high summit) is left out rather than cut.

An object layer's settings (Tool Settings, or **Settings…** in its menu) list its objects one a row, as WorldPainter's object layer dialog does: a click picks one to edit, **Shift+Click** or **Shift+↑/↓** picks a run of them, **⌘+Click** (Ctrl+Click) adds or takes one out, **⌘A** picks them all. Under the list, what those picked have: a change there goes to each of them, and a setting they do not share says **mixed**. Each object has its **Frequency** (how often it stands against the others), its **Origin** (the block set on the ground, **Auto** to find it again) and, with **Own rules** on, every one of WorldPainter's object attributes: **Turned** (at random or fixed), **Mirrored** (at random, never, always), **Height** over the ground or fixed, **Raised by** and **Varied by**, where it may stand (**On land**, **On air**, **Under water**, **On water**, **Collides with the floor**, **Under lava**, **On lava**, **Extends its foundation**), what it **Collides with** over the ground (all blocks, solid ones, none), which of its blocks **Replace underground** (all, solid, none), its **Leaves** (as they are, decay, never decay), whether it is **Waterlogged** by the water, **Joins fences and panes**, and a block **Placed as air**, to carve a hollow. Without own rules an object stands as its layer says. **Take out of the layer** removes those picked from the layer. An object two layers share is copied when one of them changes it. **Settings…** opens a wide window for layers of hundreds of objects: the list on the left, as tall as the window, with a search and a sort (Imported, Name, Height, Frequency); the layer's settings and those of the objects picked on the right. Drag its corner to resize it; it keeps its size. **New size…** in an object's **Tree size** list, and **New size** under **Density of each size**, make a tree size of your own right there.

## Structures
**Structures** (Decor group) places villages and the game's own structures, read from your own game (and your mods') and assembled as the game assembles them. Pick one in Tool Settings — under **Villages**, a theme of the game's or of a builder's pack (**Add a pack…**, described in the guide's Village packs page); under **The game's structures**, an outpost, a temple, a witch hut, an igloo, a shipwreck, ocean ruins, an Ancient City, Trial Chambers, a stronghold, trail ruins, a bastion — then **click on the map** to place it (a village of the **Size** chosen, centred on the click) or **drag a rectangle** for a village over it. No selection is needed; with one, **Fill the selection** builds a village in it. **Click one placed to select it**: drag it to move it, drag a village's corners to resize it (its houses follow the land at the same density), change its **Size** (how close its houses stand: a small village's few and far apart, a large one's many and close), **Houses** (worked out from its area unless you type a number), **Roads**, **Walls** (none, a fence, a hedge, a low wall, a palisade or ramparts, each pictured in its menu, running round its buildings with gates where the roads cross) or **Draw** under **Selected**, or click another theme in the list — it is built again — or **Take it away** (right-click does too). Each is one step to undo, and a village built again or taken away gives the land back as it was. **Building** (beside it) changes one building of a pack's village: click it to select it, drag it to move it (a path is laid from its door to the nearest road), **R** turns it about its middle, **Delete** takes it away, and Tool Settings puts another of the pack in its place from a menu of pictures, draws another of its type, or puts it back **As drawn**; the village keeps each change when it is built again with the same draw. Those placed at a depth (Ancient City, Trial Chambers, stronghold) go at the **Height** set. Their chests keep their loot tables, spawners their mobs, villages their villagers, and the Ancient City's shriekers summon the warden.

# Water
- **River** lays a river from a click to another: its bed, its banks and its water. Each click on the land adds a point exactly where you click, at the end (the points laid stay where they are), and the river ends at the last one; a click on the river puts a point between the two that stretch joins; the points are numbered, the source blue, the end dark. Drag a point's handle to move it alone; right-click it to take it away. It runs through every point and ends at the last, near the way shown while dragging, and what is dug is what was shown. Its course has no loops and no straight runs along the grid, and meanders gently on the flat; it widens downstream as the land draining into it grows, over some thirty blocks where a valley joins it; its bed is flat and wide on gentle ground and a narrow V on steep ground, its banks sloping down to it as one smooth valley; down slopes over some 30 % it falls from pool to pool, a rounded plunge pool under each fall, and on gentler slopes it runs down in rapids, a block at a time; its shallows and the inside of its bends are sand, and into the sea its channel goes on under the water, fading into the sea floor.
- **Water height** (Tool Settings), for the **Water brush** and **Flood** each: **Where I start** (the brush: as high as where each stroke starts, the water's surface there or a block over the ground) or **Where I click** (Flood, until you pick another: the water poured there runs down into the hollow below, or fills the hollow clicked, up to where it would spill over its rim), **Sea level**, or **Fixed**, a level of your own; a line under the choice says what will happen, and over the map a tag by the cursor says what a click does (**Fills to y 74**, **Fill to y 63**, **Runs down into a hollow: fills it to y 104**, with a ring where it goes, **Raise this water to y 64**) or why the ground stays dry. Flood clicked on land as high as the Fixed or sea level fills the hollow the water runs into instead, and says so. **Fixed** is the water's level, not a limit: the only limit is the folded **Height limit** (**Limit the water's height**, off by default), which every water tool keeps under.
- **Flood** puts water where you click, at any height: in the hollow clicked, or on a slope or a hilltop in the hollow the water runs down into (puddles of fewer than a dozen columns run past), or into a lake that is not full, up to its rim; where the water would run away (into the sea, off the map, out on wide flat ground), it fills the nearest hollow within 64 blocks; only with no hollow near does it ask first how far the water would spread (a block over the spot, wherever the land lies lower), with **Flood anyway**, or floods at once with **Warn before flooding beyond a hollow** off. Clicked on water, whatever the Water height, it raises it a block (asking first at the rim of its hollow, and stopping at the Height limit when it is on: **Height limit y 80 reached**); the Water height only sets the level of a new fill on dry land; its right button lowers it a block, the shore left as the ground was, and **Shift + right click** drains it whole (Flood took over from the Drain tool). Held down, either button keeps going a block at a time (**Hold speed** in Tool Settings sets how many a second), up to the rim, the height limit or the top of the world, or down to the bottom, all of it one step to undo. When nothing fills, the message says why. A flood changes 67,108,864 columns at most, all of them or none: past that (a flood, a drain, a block more or less), the water stays as it was and the message says so, rather than leave water standing cut off where it stopped.
- **Water brush** paints water at a level.

# Levels
Levels lay rock over the ground or dig into it, each in its own layer that can be shown, hidden, reordered and deleted.
- **Solid** adds rock: overhangs, arches, floating islands.
- **Carve** removes it: caves, tunnels, pits.
**Stamp slab** adds to the active level; the right button, or **Erase slab**, takes away.

# 3D
## The 3D view
- Left drag turns the view, right drag moves it.
- Scroll flies toward the land under the cursor (down to a few blocks over it, whatever the size of the map) and goes back; `Alt` + scroll comes closer around the point the view turns about, `Shift` + scroll moves the view.
- `W` `A` `S` `D` or the arrows fly over the map, `Q` and `E` go down and up (`Shift`: faster).
- Double-click looks at the point under the cursor.
- A turn or a move let go while moving goes on a little and dies away; flying with the keys starts and stops softly; a double-click glides to the point.
- While the view moves it is drawn at a lower resolution, sharp again the moment it stops.
- Each 3D View area has its own mode (Terrain, Height, Slope, Biome, Relief, True Colour), water and camera: split one to see the map in two modes at once. Each area is remembered with the layout; a new one opens in Terrain.
- **X-ray** (the scanning eye in the header) draws the floors and ceilings of the caves under the ground through it, warm near the surface and cold deep down; walking or flying in the land shows every cave as it is.
- **Shaders** (the sun in the header) gives the 3D View the look of a shader pack: the sun's shadows cast by the hills and the trees, shade where blocks close in, warmer sunlight and the Render side's AgX colours. It stays on from one session to the next.
- **Objects** (the tree in the header) shows or hides the trees, bushes and rocks of the object layers; structures stay.
- **Density** (the grid in the header) colours the land by how many objects its layers will place, from few (blue) to many (red), with a scale in the corner (objects per 100 blocks²); the objects step aside meanwhile, so that you see how thick each forest will be before exporting.
- In the **Terrain** look every block shows its own texture, from the game and from the mods and resource packs read in the Blocks panel, with what grows on it and the shade in its corners; far off, where a block is smaller than a pixel, it blends into the colour of its texture. **Settings ▸ Graphics ▸ Game textures in the 3D views** turns it off (Low draws a flat colour a block). The textures come from the game: when no Minecraft is found (nor chosen in the Blocks panel), or the graphics card cannot draw them, the views show a flat colour a block, up close too, and a line under that switch says why.

## 3D Sculpt
3D Sculpt works with the tool chosen in the **Tools** panel, as the map does, with its **Tool Settings** and the map's brush: the one picked in the **Brushes** panel, its size, intensity and rotation set in **Brush Settings** — all shown beside the 3D Sculpt view. Its top names the tool in hand and the brush with its size and intensity; a click on the brush shows the Brushes panel (`⌘` + scroll changes the brush's size in the view too). Left button uses the tool where the cursor points, right button inverts; held down, it keeps going.
- **Raise / Lower** sculpts in 3D: it pushes the surface out or in along its slope, at its **Speed**, into overhangs and caves. **Smooth** smooths the columns as on the map: the same heights from the same stroke. What was sculpted keeps its shape when a brush, on the map or here, moves the ground under it: it rides on the ground. **Paint terrain** paints the columns under the brush and the matter sculpted there.
- The map's other brushes (Flatten, the Gradient brush, Paint biome, covers, objects, layers…) act on the columns around the point under the cursor, as on the map; the Eyedropper and Flood act at the click. Their brush, and Smooth's, shows exactly the columns it takes, laid on the land.
- A tool of the map only (selections, Pan, Road, River, annotations) says so at the top of the view, where the left button then turns the view.
The brush faces the way the land faces over its whole area, not the face of the block under the cursor: on a slope of one-block steps it lies on the slope, and Raise / Lower works straight up on slopes up to 25° (on the same columns as on the map), along the surface on cliffs and in caves. Paint terrain takes whole columns, as on the map. In 3D a brush sculpts 64 blocks around at most (a larger one works as 64: its size shows in the accent at the top of the view, and Brush Settings says so under **Size** while 3D Sculpt is shown; the map's brushes go as wide as on the map). Matter pushed out keeps the blocks it came from (a sand wall gives a sand ledge), and the ground under it keeps its own; **Paint terrain** repaints sculpted matter too. Converting a terrain graph into the ground later drops what was sculpted on the tiles it rewrites (one step to undo). A brush on the map that moves the ground under sculpted matter takes it along, and a map opened lets go of sculpted cubes of 16³ that hold nothing of their own (earlier versions made them around 3D strokes, where they kept the map's brushes from showing).

## Player
**Player**, in the 3D Sculpt toolbar, walks in the view as in Minecraft with Axiom, coming down where the middle of the view looks. It looks like the game: the textures of the game's own blocks (from the Minecraft installed here), its light and shaded corners, its sky, sun, clouds and fog; the blocks the brush takes are marked in Axiom's lavender and the block under the cursor is outlined. The bar along the bottom lists what the buttons and keys do, with the flight speed.
- The cursor free, as in Axiom's editor: left drag (or the arrow keys) looks around, right uses the tool under the cursor (`Alt` + right: its inverse), middle picks the block under the cursor for **Paint**; `Ctrl` (`⌘`) + left turns about the point under the cursor, `Ctrl` + right moves the view, `Ctrl` + middle sizes the brush; the wheel sets the flight speed.
- Click the view to lend it the mouse, as in the game (`Esc` gives it back): it looks around as it moves, right uses the tool at the crosshair (builds), left its inverse (breaks).
- `W` `A` `S` `D` walk, `Ctrl` (or `W` twice) sprints.
- `Space` jumps; twice quickly, flies: `Space` goes up, `Shift` (or `Q`) down; twice again, or down to the ground, lands.
- `Shift` (or `Q`) sneaks: crouched, you do not step off an edge.
- Walking into a step of one block jumps onto it (Minecraft's Auto-Jump).
- **Ghost** (at the bottom right, or `G`) flies through everything, as the game's spectator mode: into caves, under overhangs, along tunnels. With your eyes in the ground the view sees through it to the air beyond, a cave's walls, and the cursor and brush work on what it shows. `G` again flies out (from the first air above, if you are in the ground).
Water slows you and lets you swim, and under it the world turns blue and dim, as in the game; a fall off the map puts you back where you last stood. Turn **Player** off to get the orbit camera back.

## Diorama
The **Diorama** (Beta), in the **View** section of an area's chooser, shows the map from straight above as the game would: block by block, with the game's textures, the trees, plants, houses and other objects the export places, the water over the shallows, and a light from the north-west that makes slopes, cliffs and valleys read, every tree and building casting its shadow. Put it in any area, beside the 2D map or floating; it follows the map's edits. Several can be open at once, each with its own place, turn and Surface or Caves, kept with the layout.
- Drag to pan, the wheel to zoom down to the blocks, `Space` to come back to the map's middle, `R` turns the view a quarter (`Shift` + `R` back).
- **Caves** (or `C`) shows what lies under a height, moved with `Page Up` and `Page Down` (`Shift`: 8 blocks) or the settings' slider: the floors of caves, ravines and cave lakes in their blocks, darker the deeper; rock is near black.
- The strip at the top says what is shown, the block under the pointer (x, y, z), the chunks drawn and which way is up; the gear there holds the settings (Surface or Caves, the height, which way is up, how strong the relief is).
- The tiles are built in the background, nearest the view first: a map of 1024 × 1024 blocks takes a fraction of a second on a recent Mac. From afar it shows a small image of each tile; up close, every block.

# Render
The **Render** side draws the open map path-traced: sunlight and skylight bouncing off the land, soft shadows, water that mirrors the sky and shows the shallows' floor, glowing blocks lighting what is around them, with the game's textures and models. The map is built as an export would write it, nearest the view first. Still, the image grows clean; moving, it is drawn coarser.

Its panels are editors as on the other sides (the **Render** section of an area's chooser): **Render Viewport**, **Sun and Sky**, **Camera**, **Render Output**, **Timeline**; workspaces **Still** and **Animation**.

- The **map** button (top right of the view) shows a minimap: the camera and what it sees, the Timeline's path; a click there moves the camera, a drag slides it.
- Moves as Blender's: drag turns about the point under the pointer, **Shift-drag** slides, the wheel comes toward the pointer, double-click or **.** comes in close there, **Numpad 1 3 7** views, Shift-click focuses there. Walk: hold the right button to look around, **W A S D** to move (by place: ZQSD on AZERTY), **Q E** down and up, **Shift** faster, **Alt** slower, the wheel (while walking) for the speed; every speed by the distance to what is ahead. **Camera**: the eye's **Location** in numbers, **Lock Camera to View**, **Align Camera to View**.
- **Sun and sky**: a **Look** (Clear noon, Fair weather, Golden hour, Blue hour, Foggy morning, Night, Valley mist; the fog and mist lie on the map's valleys, whatever its height), the **Time**, **Day** and **Latitude** put the sun, **North** turns the map. A moon at night.
- A **Look** sets everything of the sky it owns (hour, sun, sky, air, haze, fog, clouds, exposure); touching a setting after makes it **Custom**, and the camera path's keyframes take the look's hour. While the view moves it is drawn at half the resolution and denoised every frame; on a Retina screen the view draws a pixel a point, as Blender's automatic pixel size.
- **Atmosphere**: **Air**, **Haze**, a **Fog** (by its **Visibility**, 40 to 4000 blocks) filling low ground first, **Shafts of light**, a **Sea around the map**. **Clouds**: cover, height, thickness, density, size, wind; they shade the land.
- **Camera**: angle, aperture, focus; the point it looks at, turn, tilt, distance; **Frame the whole map**. **Render Output**: glow, bounces, **Clamp indirect** (the most light a bounce brings: fewer bright specks), **Denoise** (on at first: the grain of the first samples smoothed away, the textures kept sharp; with Open Image Denoise installed, Blender's denoiser, as the samples settle and on saved images), **Color Management** as Blender's: **View Transform** (Standard, Khronos PBR Neutral, **AgX**, Filmic, Filmic Log, False Color, Raw), **Look**, **Exposure** in stops, **Gamma**.
- **Timeline**: move the playhead, place the camera, **Add keyframe**; again elsewhere. The view follows the playhead along a smooth path; **Play** runs it. Drag a keyframe to move it; **Remove** takes it away. Keyframes are saved with the map. Keys as in Blender: **Space** play, **I**/**K** keyframe here, **Alt+I** remove it, **← →** a frame, **↓ ↑** the previous or next keyframe, **Shift+← →** start or end, **Home** the whole map.
- Keyed settings: the diamond beside a setting (or **I** over it) keys it at the playhead, **Alt+I** removes it; it eases between keys and the video draws it. Panels in Blender's sections (Sampling, Light Paths, Color Management, Output), folded by a click on a title.
- **Animation to save**: **Render animation…** draws the camera path frame by frame (the image size, **Samples a frame**, denoised) as an **MP4 video** (macOS) or **PNG images**, with progress, time left and **Cancel**; PNG images go on where they stopped.
- **Grass**: **Blocks** (the game's) or **Blades**: a field of thin blades of grass over the grass blocks, in place of the short and tall grass, their **Density** and **Height**; slower to draw.
- **Image to save**: a size (the view's, HD, 4K, 8K), samples a pixel and **Denoise**, then **Save image…** writes a PNG, drawn a band at a time, with its progress and **Cancel**; picked in the dialog, **OpenEXR** keeps the light as traced (linear, before the view transform, half floats, above 1 where it is brighter than white), with depth, normals and albedo as layers.

Press `Shift A` (or `Tab` outside a composite) to add a node: type part of its name (in any language, or its letters in order), choose with ↑ ↓ and add it with Enter; the nodes added last come first. Drag from an output to an input to link them. `V` views the selected node in the viewport, `V` again goes back to the graph's output.

## The Terrain Generator (experimental)
The Terrain side opens on the **Generator** workspace: the terrain generator of Massif 1.2, four panels marked **Experimental**: the **Node Library** on the left, the **Generator Viewer** over the **Generator Graph**, and **Node Properties** on the right (the selected node's settings, or the graph's), each moved, swapped, tabbed, floated or closed as any panel, and working together wherever they are; the samples and templates of the home screen open there too, their graph and its preview shown, and the World side only when you go to it (Apply, or the side switch). It is Massif's only generator: a map made with the classic graph of earlier versions opens with that graph converted, node by node; when the map has a graph of its own too, that one keeps driving the map and the converted nodes wait below it, unwired, in a frame titled **From the classic graph**. Its graph ends in one **Map Output** with an input for each thing the map takes (Height, Water, Albedo, Terrain, Biome, climate, layers of rock, masks), each optional. **Live** shows the graph on the World side's map as it is edited, without writing it; **Apply** writes it, undoably.

In the graph a mouse wheel or a pinch zooms around the pointer, two fingers on a trackpad move, **⌘+**/**⌘−** zoom and **F** or a double-click on the ground shows it all; the minimap at the bottom left moves the view. In 3D the viewer draws the terrain with its sky, soft light, sea and clouds: drag to turn it, right-drag to move, **Shift**+drag to turn the sun; the bar at its foot opens the render settings, shows clouds or the sea, frames the relief and saves an image. Its menu sets a plain dark background or the sky, and the resolution (up to 8192, slow and heavy). Select nodes and press **⌘G** to make a **composite** of them: one node holding them, which a double-click opens (a breadcrumb at the top, or **Esc**, leads back). Inside, its inputs and outputs are columns at the edges, and the **⤴** beside a setting exposes it on the composite. Massif's own composites (**Alpine Range**, **Cave System**, **Village Generator**) open read only: **Edit a copy** makes the world's own. **Frames** (⌘J) gather nodes under a title, colour and note, nest, fold and move with what they hold. **M** bypasses a node (the graph takes Blender's keys, see Keys of the Generator Graph); ⌘C and ⌘V copy nodes, between worlds too. A wire an input cannot take says why, and which node to put between them. **Apply** writes everything the Map Output carries in one step you can undo: the ground window by window, the caves, cave biomes and volumes, and the villages with their roads (those the graph laid before are replaced, those placed by hand stay); Cancel leaves the map as it was.

**Mountain**, **Transform** and **Combine** build a landscape from light pieces, as in Gaea. Mountain makes one mountain in the middle of the terrain from six settings: Scale, Height, Style (Basic, Eroded, Old, Alpine, Strata), Bulk, Reduce details and Seed. Transform moves, turns, scales, stretches and deforms a relief at full detail. Combine takes any number of inputs: connect In 1 and an empty In 2 appears, each input after the first with a Mask of its own, combined in order by one mode (Blend, Add, Subtract, Max, Min, Multiply, Average, Difference, Screen, Overlay, Power).

**Erosion** carves rivers into a relief: they cut valleys that branch up every slope, the slopes between them creep and slide, and what the water carries settles on valley floors and in fans. **Duration** is how long the land has eroded, **Downcutting** deep narrow valleys or broad ones, **Erosion scale** how wide the smallest valleys are, **Sediment removal** how much of what is cut leaves rather than filling the valleys, **Rock softness** how easily the rock gives, **Base level** where nothing more is cut. Its Flow, Wear and Deposition masks colour rivers, gullies and alluvium. It works over the whole terrain, so Apply window by window leaves no seams. Graphs made with Stream erosion keep it; in MassifScript it is `Erosion@1`.

## MassifScript
The graph can be read and written as text: the **script** button at the top right of the graph shows the **Script** pane beside it. `base = FractalNoise(scale: 2400, height: 600);` makes a node, `eroded = Erosion(base);` another fed by the first, `group "Geology" { … }` a frame, `output height = eroded;` the Map Output's Height; `component`, `const`, `let`, `if`, `for` and `import` build more at once. Mistakes are underlined as you type, with their line and column. **Apply** makes the graph from the script as one step you can undo; **Graph → script** writes the graph as it is. **Copy for AI** copies a guide to paste into any AI chat, with every node and the graph open now: ask it for a terrain, paste its script back and Apply. An MCP server, for an AI assistant to work on the graph in Massif directly, is planned, not shipped. The pane opens and saves `.mscript` files; docs/massifscript.md describes the language.

## Applying
**Apply**, in the Generator Viewer, writes the graph to the map in one step you can undo. The viewer's bar keeps what you see apart from what is done to the map. The sliders button, beside **2D/3D** and **Clay/Colours**, holds the **View** options, which never touch the map: relief, contour lines, mask opacity, sun height, background and resolution. On the right, after a line: **Live**, **Apply to map**, and the arrow beside it with **Keep the map's ground** (what the graph digs and raises goes into levels of their own, the map's ground staying as it was) and **Height map: Save…** (the terrain saved as a 16-bit PNG height map, or an OpenEXR of its heights in blocks, a block a sample, without writing it). Whether the nodes' thumbnails are in clay or in colours is a setting of the graph: **Node thumbnails**, in Node Properties with no node selected. The viewer opens in 3D under the sky; with the Map Output viewed it shows the water of the graph's rivers and lakes and its layers of rock over the ground. A new map made from a template, or a sample of the home screen, has its template's graph in the Terrain Generator, applied at once.

# WorldPainter worlds
**Import from WorldPainter…** opens a `.world` file: its dimensions, heights (tall worlds keep their height), terrains, water, biomes and custom terrains (each one terrain of its blocks, laid in noise, patches or layers by height as WorldPainter's mix says). WorldPainter's **Beaches** come in as it draws them: grass with patches of sand, gravel and clay.

Its layers come too. Forests (Deciduous, Pine, Jungle, Swamp) and the trees of WorldPainter Modernised's tree pack become object layers of the same trees. Custom object layers become object layers, their objects read from the world itself (Sponge and MCEdit schematics, structures, Bo2) with their offset, frequency and every one of their attributes as their own rules. Every tree layer stands about as many trees as WorldPainter's export: the import plays WorldPainter's placement with the trees' own shapes, which may stand far closer than their size where their branches leave room, and sets the layer's spacing and density to match, at every strength painted. Every layer of a block comes in on that block, with its own intensity, as WorldPainter keeps them: a block painted with a forest at 50 %, bushes at 15 % and three ground covers keeps all of them. Covers lie over each other as WorldPainter lays them (custom ground covers on the ground, plants on them, frost over everything) and the export grows each in turn; each tree layer stands its trees thinned to its intensity there. The Info panel lists them all, with their intensities; rubbing one out leaves the others (the cover under it shows), and painting a layer over the block takes the place of all of that slot's. Plant layers become ground covers with the same plants in the same shares. **Frost**, **Lava**, **Void** (columns of nothing) and **Annotations** come in as Massif's. Caverns, chasms and caves are carved where they were painted, the more the stronger. Each tunnel layer becomes a Carve level (a floating one, a Solid level) with its floor and roof where WorldPainter puts them. A custom ground cover becomes a ground cover of its blocks, as high as it is thick, painted where it was, over the terrains as they were: the terrains stay the map's own and the cover stays a layer to paint, hide or remove. One that takes the place of the top of the land lies on the ground lowered under it. A combined layer brings the layers it holds, at their factors, and its terrain and biome, and comes in as a combined layer to paint on with. Underground pockets become pocket layers of the same blocks, as frequent and as large, between the same heights. Rivers painted with WorldPainter's River layer are dug where they were painted, as its export digs them. Ground covers whose blocks are the layers of a block (Conquest Reforged's `grass_block_layer`, snow layers, covers mixing several of them) are made the ground's own as the world is imported: before it is read, the import asks for the mods the world uses (the game's version, the mods' jars or a modpack's instance, remembered for the next time), **Make covers of layers the ground's own** (on) and **Smooth terrain** (on, and always with the first). Left off, they are listed first in the import report, ticked, whether or not Conquest Reforged is read in the Blocks panel: **Make them the ground's own** puts the cover's layers in place of the top of the land (Conquest's slabs by eighths count too), a cover of several blocks making one terrain of them in its mix, not one a block: the ground keeps its precise height, with the full block on top (eight layers of a block that has no full one), and the whole map is smoothed with layers on export, the ground with no cover too, so that the two meet level with no seam where the cover ended, as one step to undo; a mix's other blocks (small stones) stay a cover, and plants or frost painted over them stay. **Edit ▸ Covers of Layers to Ground…** does the same at any time. Custom biomes come in as biomes of the map, painted where they were and exported by their names, which are their ids (`terralith:alpine_highlands`); one named otherwise keeps its name, its id made of it. Custom annotation layers, and the layers set not to be exported (placeholders), become annotation layers of their name and colour, overlapping as in WorldPainter, each in the palette it was sorted into. Every custom layer keeps the colour WorldPainter shows it in, and a ground cover its opacity. The surface's overlay images (View ▸ Overlays) become reference images at the same place, scale and opacity, shown or hidden as they were; their files are looked for where WorldPainter had them, then by name beside the `.world`. One not found keeps its place: **Find its file…**, in the reference image's settings, links it again. The import report says what became what; it is kept with the map: **File ▸ Import Report…** shows it again.

**Import WorldPainter layer…** (top of the Layers panel, Experimental) reads `.layer` files, custom layers exported from WorldPainter, several at once; dropping them on the window does the same. Plant layers, custom object layers (Bo2, schematics, structures), ground covers, underground pockets and combined layers become Massif's layers, the first picked to paint with; tunnel, floating and custom annotation layers are not carried over. A report says what each became and what was left out (a ground cover's edges and noise, farmland). Objects keep all their attributes. Layers of the map are never changed: one with a layer's name comes in as a layer of its own.

# Scripts
**File ▸ Run Script…** runs a script on the map: one written for Massif's own API in Lua (`.lua`: the map is `massif.map`, read and written by rectangles of columns, masks painted with `map:import_mask`, Edit ▸ Whole map's passes with `map:whole_map`, its own terrains, ground covers and object layers made with `map:add_terrain`, `map:add_cover` and `map:add_objects`, its spawn moved with `map.spawn`), or one of WorldPainter's JavaScript scripts as it is (`.js`: its `wp` object, its masks, layers, terrains, filters and worlds), its `world` the open map and its `dimension` the one shown. The dialog asks for the parameters the script names at its head and its arguments, one a line; **Run** runs it in the background, what it prints showing as it comes and an error with its line and column, a bar showing how far it is, **Stop** stopping it. What it changed is one step to undo; a script that fails or is stopped leaves the map as it was. **Run again** reads the file again. A script may only use files in its own folder, those picked as its parameters, and Minecraft's saves; it has no network. What Massif does not do yet stops the script with "… is not supported yet". On the command line: `massif-cli script my.lua arguments…`. docs/scripting.md describes the API.

# Settings
**Settings…** (`⌘,`: in the **Massif** menu, or the home screen's sidebar) has ten sections.
- **Appearance**: the theme, the accent colour, the loader shown while Massif works (a grid of dots in one of eight patterns), the tool icons (Line or Classic), the crosshair of the player's view and of the middle of the map view (a dot, a cross, a circle, Minecraft's plus or none, its size and opacity, with a preview), and last the **Home background** (Ridges, Waves, Pixel or Prism, with a live picture and its few settings) and **Animate the background**.
- **Typeface**: what the interface is set in — the system's own typeface, or Inter, Geist, IBM Plex Sans or Manrope, built into Massif. Each is shown in itself; a click sets the whole interface in it.
- **Sounds**: little sounds for what happens, made by Massif as it plays them. **Essential**: tasks done, saving, errors. **All**: the interface too (choices, switches, windows, workspaces). Each can be heard from there. The sound output is held only while a sound plays: headphones are free to go to another device.
- **Notifications**: the system tells when a long task ends (an export, an import, a conversion, a pass over the whole map): never, while Massif is in the background, or always.
- **Memory**: a limit for what Massif keeps decoded to be quick (the map's tiles, the map view's images), and what is in use now. Past it, what was used least is put away, compressed.
- **Autosave**: a copy of the map while it has changes not saved, every 1, 2, 5, 10 or 15 minutes; or none.
- **Updates**: **Check for new versions** (on by default): once a day at most, Massif asks GitHub whether a newer version is out, sending nothing about you or your maps. If one is, a pill in the top bar (and a line above Settings on the home screen) opens its notes and a button to its page, in your browser; Massif never downloads or installs anything itself. **Skip this version** stops telling of that one.
- **Graphics**: **Low**, **Medium** or **High** (or **Custom**, any mix): the 3D views' render scale and smoothing, fog, water reflections, the game's textures in the 3D views, how much of the map the 3D view keeps on the graphics card (a larger map is drawn coarser, and much faster), the Terrain viewport's light and clouds, the map view's images on the graphics card, the frames a second of an idle window, and what the views load: **Around the view** (the default: a 3D view builds within its **View distance** of the camera, the Diorama what it shows and a margin, the Render the part of the map within the view distance of the point its camera looks at, and all rest once it is ready) or **The whole map**. Changes apply at once. The first start picks a preset for the graphics card; when the 3D views stay slow (not while their tiles are being built), a note suggests one step lower, with a button that applies it; its × stops the suggestion for good.
- **Developer**: developer mode offers a **Developer** panel in every area's menu: frames a second and the time of each, the memory the system counts, the processor's time, in live charts; what is kept decoded, the map, its tasks and the graphics card. **Frames a second** keeps the window in step with the screen (**Vsync**, the default) or lets it draw up to 60, 120, 144, 240 frames a second or **Unlimited**; **Draw every frame** keeps it drawing when nothing changes, to measure.

# Reporting a problem
**Help ▸ Report a Problem…** makes one `.zip` file to send us when Massif crashes or is slow: in **#bug-reports** on Massif's Discord, or with an issue on GitHub; the dialog links to both. Nothing is sent from Massif: you save it and send it yourself.
- It holds what happened in your words, Massif's version, the computer and its graphics card, the settings that change how Massif draws, the map's size, the last minute of the **Developer** panel's figures, and the logs of the last two starts. The map itself goes in only if **Include the map** is on.
- Every file is listed, and **Show** opens it as it will be written. Your home folder is written `~`, and other accounts' names are left out.
- **Copy Summary** puts a few lines on the clipboard for your message.
- For slowness, **Start Recording**, do the slow thing, then **Stop** at the top of the window: each frame and what took its time go in the report.
- After Massif closed without saving, the dialog that offers your map back has **Report the Problem…** too.

## Recording performance
When Massif stutters, **Help ▸ Record Performance…** (or **Record…** in **Settings ▸ Developer**) records it for us. **Start Recording**, use Massif as when it slows down, then click the red dot at the right of the top bar (or **Help ▸ Stop Recording Performance**).
- The recording is saved on your desktop as `Massif performance <date>.zip` and shown in the Finder: send it in **#bug-reports**. It stops by itself after ten minutes.
- It holds every frame and where its time went (the map, the 3D views, the Diorama, the Render side, drawing, waiting for the screen), the memory, the processor, your computer and graphics card, the settings that change how Massif draws, the map's size, the workspaces, and the pointer, the keys and the tool in hand as you used them.
- Never the map itself, nor what you type, nor where your files are.

## If Massif does not start
Massif writes every step of its start in `startup.log` (on Windows in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Massif`, on macOS in `~/Library/Logs/Massif`, on Linux in `~/.local/state/massif`), the start before in `startup.previous.log`. Once Massif starts again, **Help ▸ Report a Problem…** puts them in the report; if it never starts, send those two files. A graphics API whose driver got stuck is set aside in `graphics.txt` beside them: delete it to try them all again.
